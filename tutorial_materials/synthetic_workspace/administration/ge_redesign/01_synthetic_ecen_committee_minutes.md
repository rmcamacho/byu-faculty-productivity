# Synthetic ECEN Curriculum Committee Minutes

> **SYNTHETIC TUTORIAL DOCUMENT — NOT AN OFFICIAL BYU OR ECEN RECORD**

**Meeting date:** August 20, 2026
**Meeting type:** Special curriculum discussion
**Participants:** Department chair, undergraduate coordinator, assessment coordinator, four fictional faculty representatives, and an academic adviser. Names are intentionally omitted.

## Purpose

Discuss the revised General Education proposal and prepare recommendations for the fictional college curriculum committee.

## Discussion Summary

The committee generally supported a simpler GE structure and clearer program-level assessment. Members appreciated the attempt to create a common BYU experience while preserving some choice in the exploration courses.

The committee raised five engineering-specific questions:

1. What mathematical preparation will be expected in UNIV 106: Numeracy and Data?
2. Will engineering students repeat material already covered in calculus, statistics, or engineering-computation courses?
3. How will AI literacy be divided between GE and disciplinary courses?
4. Can engineering faculty contribute sections organized around engineering examples while meeting common outcomes?
5. What teaching load and student-credit-hour arrangements would accompany ECEN participation?

One member proposed allowing the fictional **ECEN 230: Engineering Data and Decisions** course to substitute for UNIV 106. Another member noted that this appeared inconsistent with the redesign's effort to prevent major courses from substituting for GE. The committee did not resolve the issue.

The assessment coordinator recommended volunteering one faculty member for a future UNIV 106 working group and one for UNIV 105: Stewardship and the Natural World. The chair responded that ECEN should volunteer only after the college explains the workload and how teaching credit will be assigned.

## Recorded Decisions

- Support continued development of the revised proposal.
- Do not endorse or oppose final adoption until course-level outcomes and resource expectations are clearer.
- Ask the college to seek clarification about UNIV 106 preparation, major-course substitutions, faculty workload, and AI competencies.
- Prepare a short list of ECEN faculty expertise potentially relevant to UNIV 105, UNIV 106, and UNIV 109.
- Review whether current ECEN major requirements could change if UNIV 106 provides meaningful quantitative and data preparation.
