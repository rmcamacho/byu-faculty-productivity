## Official Source

- [BYU General Education Redesign](https://ge.byu.edu/geredesign)
- Open comment period shown on the redesign page: **August 13–31, 2026**.

## High-Level Proposal Facts

The current revision describes a 26-credit GE program for newly admitted, non-transfer students. It retains the current religious-education structure and organizes GE into nine UNIV courses:

- **BYU Foundations:** UNIV 101, a two-credit first-semester course graded credit/no credit.
- **BYU Cornerstones:** UNIV 102–105, four required three-credit interdisciplinary courses without substitutions.
- **BYU Explorations:** UNIV 106–109, four three-credit requirements with multiple sections, emphases, or levels. Qualifying AP or transfer work may fulfill these requirements.

The proposal identifies the exploration areas as numeracy and data; effective communication; languages and cultures; and arts, letters, or sciences.

## Design Themes Relevant To Engineering

- GE is intended to be coherent, programmatic, assessable, and easier for students to navigate.
- The redesign seeks to eliminate double-counting between GE and majors because of path dependency, uneven student experiences, and assessment difficulties.
- Major-specific courses are not presently proposed as substitutes for UNIV exploration courses, although colleges and departments may need to adjust major requirements after learning what the GE courses teach.
- The proposal combines shared outcomes with room for faculty expertise and section-level variation.
- Faculty feedback has raised questions about student choice, disciplinary rigor, prior credit, faculty autonomy, staffing, classrooms, scheduling, and resources.
- Feedback also raised AI and emerging-technology competencies. The current response suggests that technology varies substantially by discipline and may often be addressed most effectively within disciplinary courses.
- Proposed governance places program-level oversight in Undergraduate Education. Academic units would contribute instructors, and student credit hours would be credited to the contributing units.
- Course details and course-level outcomes remain preliminary. Representative faculty working groups would develop them if the proposal advances.
- No implementation date has been established. The proposal anticipates a multi-year transition if approved.

## Status And Process

The redesign has gone through multiple rounds of faculty, staff, college, department, council, and administrative review. The August 2026 materials invite further comments. The official history page anticipates that a further revision could be considered by the Undergraduate Curriculum Council in fall 2026. Approval, working-group formation, course development, and implementation remain future steps rather than completed decisions.
