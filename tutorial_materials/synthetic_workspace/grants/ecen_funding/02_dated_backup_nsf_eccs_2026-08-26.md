## Confirmed Opportunity Facts as of the Check Date

- NSF posted NSF 26-514 on August 17, 2026 and identified it as the active current solicitation.
- Full proposals are accepted anytime; there is no fixed program deadline stated in the solicitation.
- The opportunity covers three ECCS programs:
  - Electronic, Photonic, Magnetic, and Quantum Devices (EPMQD);
  - Circuits and Systems for Communications and Sensing (CSCS);
  - Energy, Power, Control, and Learning (EPCL).
- Letters of intent and preliminary proposals are not required.
- The solicitation states no PI restrictions and no organizational or PI proposal-count limits.
- Voluntary committed cost sharing is prohibited.
- No program-specific indirect-cost or other budget limitation is stated. Budget and duration must be appropriate to the proposed scope.
- NSF anticipated a portfolio with a range of budgets and durations, estimated 245–300 awards, and an anticipated total program amount of $110 million, subject to available funds. Those portfolio figures are not a promised award size.
- Proposals follow the PAPPG and may be submitted through Research.gov or Grants.gov. Separately submitted collaborative proposals must use Research.gov.
- Proposals are reviewed using NSF Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts criteria.
- Investigators are strongly encouraged to email a one-page project summary to the relevant program director well before full submission to discuss scope. That conversation does not constitute review or influence the funding decision.

## Program-Scope Notes

### CSCS

The CSCS program supports foundational research in communications, sensing, circuits, and signal processing. The program page includes analog and mixed-signal integrated circuits, hardware–algorithm connections, energy-efficient system components, sensing systems, and machine learning that materially advances signal processing or circuit design.

### EPMQD

EPMQD centers on devices whose new capabilities arise from structure and material properties. It includes electronic, photonic, magnetic, and quantum devices, including physical and chemical sensing devices. A proposal that is primarily a circuit or system method should not be moved into EPMQD merely because it uses a sensor.

### EPCL

EPCL supports foundational work in control, learning, optimization, networked systems, power, energy, and engineered-system resilience. A proposal should advance the underlying theory or method; incidental use of AI is not enough.
