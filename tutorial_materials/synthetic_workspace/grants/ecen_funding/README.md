# ECEN Funding Search and Proposal-Concept Exercise

> **SANITIZED FACULTY-TUTORIAL WORKSPACE — NO LIVE PROPOSAL OR CONFIDENTIAL RESEARCH DATA**

This exercise shows how to move from faculty intent to a verified funding opportunity and then to a reviewable proposal concept. It does not authorize submission, institutional commitments, or changes in Kuali.

Participants may use their own research direction or the synthetic profile in this folder. A Google Scholar profile is optional. Complete the intent-first workflow in `00_research_profile_intake.md` before searching.

## Intended Time: 11–13 Minutes

This is a fast demonstration of the workflow, not a complete proposal-development session.

## Path A — Use Your Own Research Direction

Begin with:

```text
Help me prepare to search for research funding, but do not search yet. Ask me no more than four concise questions about my current research, any particular direction I want to pursue next, the capabilities and collaborators I can realistically use, and the desired project scale or sponsor constraints. Then summarize my answers as a provisional funding-search brief and ask me to correct it.
```

If desired, supply a public Google Scholar URL only after stating the future direction. Ask Codex to use the profile as supporting evidence rather than letting older publications dictate the search.

## Path B — Use the Synthetic Faculty Profile

Ask Codex to read `00_synthetic_faculty_direction.md` and `01_synthetic_constraints.md`, then produce a one-page funding-search brief. It must separate the desired direction, existing capabilities, synthetic assumptions, and unresolved questions.

## Step 1 — Search Live Opportunities

```text
Using the approved funding-search brief, find up to five active funding opportunities that appear relevant today. Search official sponsor and Grants.gov pages, not listicles or funding-database summaries. For each candidate, open the current opportunity page and report:

- sponsor, program, and official URL;
- active status and the date you verified it;
- exact deadline or “accepted anytime” language;
- applicant and PI eligibility;
- topical fit;
- award amount and duration, or “not specified”;
- required partners, letters, preproposals, or cost sharing;
- the strongest fit reason;
- the strongest concern or unresolved question.

Distinguish confirmed facts from inference. Exclude expired opportunities. Do not draft a proposal, contact a sponsor, or enter anything in Kuali.
```

## Step 2 — Challenge the Shortlist

```text
Rank the candidates using technical fit, eligibility confidence, time feasibility, project-scale fit, and strategic value. Treat any unverified eligibility, deadline, budget, or program-scope claim as a blocker rather than filling the gap. Recommend one opportunity to investigate further and explain why the others should wait or be rejected. Cite the official source for every decisive fact.
```

If live search is unavailable, use `02_dated_backup_nsf_eccs_2026-08-26.md`. First ask Codex to audit `03_deliberately_flawed_shortlist.md` against that source.

## Step 3 — Draft a Concept, Not a Submission

```text
Using the approved research-direction brief and the verified opportunity, draft a one-page proposal concept using the structure in 04_concept_note_template.md. Ground every capability claim in the supplied files or label it as needing confirmation. Keep the project commensurate with the opportunity and identify the most important technical risk, validation test, student role, broader impact, and sponsor-fit question. Do not invent preliminary results, facilities, partners, budget rates, or institutional commitments. Do not create or update a Kuali record.
```

## Step 4 — Instructor or Faculty Review

Before using the concept outside the workshop, verify:

- the opportunity is still active;
- the exact solicitation and amendments are current;
- institutional and PI eligibility;
- the selected program or track;
- page, budget, duration, and document requirements;
- feasibility of every method, facility, partner, and student role;
- alignment with the faculty member's intended direction;
- whether a program officer conversation is recommended.

The faculty member owns the scientific direction. BYU research administrators and authorized officials own institutional process, approval, and submission decisions.
