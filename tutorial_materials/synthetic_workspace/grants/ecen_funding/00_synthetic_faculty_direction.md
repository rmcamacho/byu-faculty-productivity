# Synthetic Faculty Research Direction

## Faculty Description

I work on low-power analog and mixed-signal circuits for sensing systems. My prior work has included sensor interfaces, calibration methods, embedded signal processing, and laboratory prototypes for environmental and infrastructure measurements.

For the next several years, I would like to investigate trustworthy adaptive sensor interfaces that can maintain useful measurements when sensors drift, operating conditions change, or communication and energy are constrained. I am interested in hardware–algorithm co-design rather than applying a generic machine-learning model after data collection.

I would like the work to produce foundational circuit and signal-processing knowledge while still having a credible path to field-relevant validation. I am particularly interested in projects that give undergraduate and graduate students meaningful roles in circuit design, measurement, modeling, and reproducible evaluation.

## Desired Direction

- Develop low-power interfaces that detect or compensate for changing sensor behavior.
- Study which adaptation should occur in analog hardware, embedded computation, or later processing.
- Establish measurable relationships among energy, calibration accuracy, robustness, and explainability.
- Validate first with controlled emulation and benchtop prototypes before claiming field deployment.
