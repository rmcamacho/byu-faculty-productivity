# Faculty-Intent and Research-Profile Intake for Grant Searching

Use this exercise before searching for funding opportunities. The faculty member's intended direction is the authoritative starting point. A publication profile is optional supporting evidence; it should not define the faculty member or choose a proposal direction for them.

## Step 1 — Start With Faculty Intent

Every participant begins here, whether or not they have a Google Scholar profile.

```text
Help me prepare to search for research funding, but do not search for opportunities yet. First ask me no more than six concise questions to understand:

- my current research area;
- any particular direction I want to take the research next;
- the problem or impact I care about;
- methods, facilities, data, or capabilities I can realistically use;
- likely collaborators and student roles;
- preferred project scale, timing, sponsors, and exclusions.

Do not infer my future direction from my publication history before hearing my answer. After I respond, summarize my answers as a provisional research-direction brief and list any consequential uncertainty that remains.
```

The participant can answer conversationally. A useful answer might be only a paragraph:

```text
My current work is in [area]. For the next several years I am especially interested in [direction or problem]. I can realistically use [methods, facilities, data, or collaborators]. I would like a project that supports [student roles or outcomes] over approximately [time and scale]. I want to include or avoid [sponsors, topics, partners, or constraints].
```

The research direction may be an extension of prior work, a deliberate pivot, or an early idea not yet represented in publications. Codex should preserve that distinction.

## Step 2 — Choose Optional Evidence

A Google Scholar profile is helpful but not required.

Preferred starting source:

- the faculty member's public Google Scholar profile URL.

Useful corroborating sources:

- current CV or biosketch;
- public BYU faculty profile;
- ORCID record;
- laboratory or personal research website;
- a faculty-selected publication list.

If none of these sources is available, continue directly from the faculty member's description. Codex can still create a search brief, but it must label claims about track record, facilities, preliminary results, and team capability as unverified unless the faculty member supplies evidence.

A Google Scholar profile link works for other viewers only if its owner has made the profile public. Google explains public profile visibility and link format in its [Google Scholar Profiles documentation](https://scholar.google.com/intl/en/scholar/citations.html).

## Privacy Boundary

Use only public professional information or files the faculty member intentionally places in the tutorial workspace. Do not add unpublished manuscripts, confidential reviews, student records, export-controlled details, or unannounced intellectual property.

## Step 3A — With a Google Scholar Profile

```text
My approved research-direction brief is [paste brief or give its filename]. Review my public Google Scholar profile at [paste profile URL] as supporting evidence. Do not modify any online account or file, and do not let older publication themes override the direction I stated.

Produce:

1. areas where my stated direction is supported by prior publications;
2. relevant methods, platforms, or application experience visible in the record;
3. adjacent prior themes that might strengthen the direction without changing it;
4. gaps between the desired direction and the published record;
5. terminology that funding agencies might use for this work;
6. an evidence table with representative publication titles and years;
7. uncertainties, possible misattributions, and questions I must answer.

Distinguish facts explicitly shown in the profile from your inferences. Do not rank research quality from citation counts, infer private capabilities, or assume that every coauthored publication represents an active future direction. Do not search for grants yet.
```

## Step 3B — Without a Google Scholar Profile

```text
Use my answers and the provisional research-direction brief to create a funding-search profile. Organize it into the desired future direction, motivating problem, relevant current expertise, methods and resources, collaborators and student roles, project scale, sponsor vocabulary, exclusions, and unanswered questions.

Treat my statements as self-reported. Do not invent publications, preliminary results, facilities, partners, or institutional commitments. Mark any claim that would need evidence before it appears in a proposal. Do not search for grants yet.
```

This route is fully sufficient for the opportunity-search exercise. A Scholar profile is not a prerequisite.

## Step 4 — Faculty Review: Required Pause

The faculty member should correct the brief before Codex continues. In particular, mark:

- the future direction that should drive the search;
- prior themes that remain relevant;
- themes that are complete or should not influence the search;
- methods and facilities genuinely available to the proposed team;
- new directions not yet represented by publications;
- topics that should not be included in funding searches;
- desired project scale, collaborators, student roles, and sponsor constraints.

Suggested reply:

```text
Revise the research-direction brief using these corrections: [paste corrections]. Treat my corrections as authoritative. If publication evidence was provided, preserve a short table showing which titles support each retained capability or theme. If no publication evidence was provided, label track-record claims as self-reported rather than inventing citations. Save the result as corrected_research_profile.md. Do not search for grants yet.
```

## Step 5 — Verify Evidence When Available

```text
For each retained capability or theme that is relevant to my desired direction, select no more than two to four representative publications. Verify the title, author list, year, venue, and DOI or stable publisher URL using primary publication sources where possible. Flag any bibliographic detail you cannot verify. Google Scholar snippets and citation counts are discovery aids, not final citation sources. Save the results as representative_publications.md.
```

Only the small set of publications that directly supports a later proposal claim should move into a proposal bibliography. A paper should not be cited merely because it belongs to the investigator.

Skip this publication-verification step when the participant has not supplied a publication record. It can be completed later if a promising opportunity requires evidence of prior work.

## Step 6 — Prepare the Opportunity-Search Brief

```text
Using corrected_research_profile.md and representative_publications.md if it exists, draft a one-page funding-search brief. Put my desired future direction first. Include the motivating problem, relevant expertise, distinctive capabilities, desired project scale, plausible sponsor vocabulary, exclusions, and unresolved questions. Separate verified evidence, self-reported information, and inference. Do not invent facilities, preliminary results, collaborators, or institutional commitments. Do not search for opportunities yet.
```

The approved search brief becomes the input for the live grant-opportunity search. This pause prevents a noisy publication list from driving an irrelevant search.

## If Google Scholar Is Unavailable

Google Scholar may block automated access, require a CAPTCHA, or the faculty profile may be private. Do not bypass those controls. Use one of these fallbacks:

1. Continue from the faculty member's own research-direction description.
2. Ask the faculty member to provide a current CV or biosketch if useful.
3. Use the public BYU profile, ORCID record, and laboratory website together.
4. Ask the faculty member to export or paste a selected publication list.

The same research-direction and faculty-correction prompts work with those sources.

## What This Demonstrates

- eliciting the faculty member's desired direction before searching;
- working productively without a publication profile;
- using public records to support rather than dictate direction;
- separating evidence from inference;
- giving the subject of the profile editorial control;
- focusing a live funding search without overfitting to publication keywords;
- verifying citations before proposal drafting.
