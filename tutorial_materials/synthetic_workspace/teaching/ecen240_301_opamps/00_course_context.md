# Course Context

> **SANITIZED FACULTY-TUTORIAL COPY — NOT THE LIVE COURSE**

ECEn 240/301 is a combined undergraduate circuits course. Students entering the operational-amplifier unit have practiced Ohm's law, Kirchhoff's laws, node-voltage analysis, Thevenin equivalents, and basic first-order circuit reasoning.

The op-amp unit introduces an important engineering abstraction: students analyze terminal behavior without first studying the transistor-level implementation. The unit prepares them to recognize and analyze several uses of op-amps:

- comparing two voltages;
- buffering a source from a load;
- amplifying and combining signals;
- converting or conditioning signals for measurement systems.

This workshop stays focused on ideal op-amp reasoning. It does not require transistor-level design, frequency response, slew rate, input offset, or photodiode circuits.

## Instructional Pattern

The course uses short concept explanations, worked circuit examples, individual practice, homework, and later laboratory application. Students are expected to state assumptions, write equations, compute a result, and check whether the assumed operating mode is physically consistent.

## Boundaries for New Material

- Keep each new problem solvable in about 10–15 minutes.
- Use only concepts listed in the learning outcomes and lecture summary.
- Make every circuit fully understandable from text; do not depend on an unavailable figure.
- Use original numbers and scenarios rather than reproducing textbook problems.
- Provide a complete instructor solution.
- Check requested output voltage against the stated supply rails.
- Do not treat the virtual short as a physical connection or apply it without checking its conditions.
