# Facilitator Key: ECEN Grant-Development Exercise

> **FACILITATOR MATERIAL — KEEP OUTSIDE THE PARTICIPANT WORKSPACE**

## Core Lesson

The impressive step is not generating a polished proposal paragraph. It is constructing a traceable chain:

```text
faculty intent → evidence → current opportunity → eligibility and scope → concept → compliance and administrative planning
```

Each arrow is a place where plausible AI output can become wrong.

## Audit of the Deliberately Flawed Shortlist

The draft contains these planted problems:

1. **Wrong deadline.** NSF 26-514 accepts proposals anytime. October 9, 2026 belongs to NSF ERI, not the ECCS core solicitation.
2. **Invented award size and duration.** NSF 26-514 does not promise exactly $200,000 for 24 months. Those limits describe ERI. ECCS says budgets and durations must be commensurate with scope.
3. **Unsupported program selection.** A sensor application does not automatically belong in EPMQD. The synthetic concept is presently more aligned with CSCS because its novelty is at circuit/system and signal-processing level. Device-level novelty could change that conclusion.
4. **Invented letter of intent.** NSF 26-514 does not require an LOI or preliminary proposal.
5. **Ignored program contact guidance.** The solicitation strongly encourages a one-page summary and discussion with the relevant program director well before submission.
6. **Invented facility commitment.** The synthetic constraints explicitly say fabrication access is unconfirmed. Codex may propose it as an option or question, not a fact.
7. **Prohibited cost sharing.** Voluntary committed cost sharing is prohibited.
8. **Unsafe ERI eligibility conclusion.** ERI has restrictive institutional and PI eligibility. BYU's current Carnegie research designation and the solicitation's controlling classification language must be reconciled with BYU research administrators and, if needed, the sponsor; a quick search is not sufficient authorization to proceed.
9. **Premature Kuali action.** Opportunity fit, eligibility, faculty direction, project scope, and internal planning should be resolved before creating a live record merely because a program exists.
10. **Invented partner and reversed design sequence.** The faculty member must choose and justify the validation application. The assistant cannot name a field partner without evidence or authorization.

## Good Live-Search Output

A strong answer:

- uses official sponsor pages and current solicitations;
- records the verification date;
- separates deadline from internal planning time;
- labels missing award information as unspecified;
- treats eligibility uncertainty as a blocker;
- distinguishes topical resemblance from programmatic fit;
- recommends no more than one or two opportunities for deeper review;
- does not draft or open Kuali before the faculty member chooses.

## Strong Concept Characteristics

For the synthetic profile, a promising concept would explore a foundational question such as how adaptation should be partitioned across analog interfaces and embedded computation under measurable energy, drift, and uncertainty constraints. It should:

- use benchtop emulation and prototypes as the confirmed first validation stage;
- avoid depending on custom fabrication or a field site;
- compare against a defined static-calibration or post-processing baseline;
- identify a measurable tradeoff or limit rather than promise a generic “smart sensor”;
- give students technically substantive design, measurement, and reproducibility roles;
- ask the CSCS program team whether the proposed hardware–algorithm contribution is sufficiently foundational and in scope.

## Weak Concept Warning Signs

- “Use AI to improve sensors” without a new research question.
- A product description instead of a knowledge contribution.
- Field deployment promised before a validated benchtop mechanism exists.
- Fabrication, data, partners, or preliminary results invented from aspiration.
- A budget chosen because it appeared in another solicitation.
- Broader impacts reduced to generic student participation.

## Time-Control Guidance

For an 11–13 minute exercise:

- spend 2 minutes clarifying faculty intent;
- spend 4 minutes on live search or backup-source audit;
- spend 2 minutes selecting and challenging one match;
- spend 3–5 minutes drafting and reviewing the concept skeleton.

Stop at a concept skeleton. Compliance extraction, budget development, red-team review, and Kuali preparation are later workflows that can be shown briefly but should not be completed during this hour.
